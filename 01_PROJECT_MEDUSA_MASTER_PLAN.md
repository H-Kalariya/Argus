# Project Medusa: Physical Challenge-Response Deepfake & Fraud-Ring Defense

## The Core Thesis
We don't chase the deepfake-detection arms race pixel-by-pixel. We force every payment-flow attacker — human or AI-driven — through short physical challenges that live face-swap pipelines cannot survive intact, we measure the exact degradation signature those pipelines leave behind, and we correlate every failure with a tiered identity graph that catches both bot-speed attacks and slow-moving mule networks. Detection, explainability, and network defense in one gated payment flow.

This is not a speculative idea — it builds directly on **GOTCHA: Real-Time Video Deepfake Detection via Challenge-Response** (Mittal, Hegde, Memon — NYU, arXiv:2210.06186), which proved that a taxonomy of active/passive challenges reliably degrades state-of-the-art real-time deepfake generators, reaching 88.6% human-judged AUC and 80.1% automated-scorer AUC. Medusa adapts that academic result into a buildable, demoable payment-fraud product, fuses it with a tiered identity graph, and gates it through Razorpay's payment flow.

## Part 1: The Challenge Engine (What the User Sees)

**Design principle:** trivially easy for a human, mathematically punishing for a real-time face-swap model.

| Category | Example prompt | Why it breaks face-swap |
|---|---|---|
| Self-occlusion | "Touch your chin with your right palm, hold 3s" | Hand covers ~40% of face; swap model loses landmark lock; mask smears |
| Extreme angular rotation | "Turn head fully left, then look up" | Landmarks collapse near 90° profile; reenactment jitters |
| Foreground object insertion | "Hold your phone over half your face" | Destroys the tracker's bounding box; swap model "swims" |
| Rapid motion | "Sweep your hand across your face, left to right" | High-velocity occlusion causes edge flicker and mask-boundary instability |

Challenges are drawn from a **curated, parameterized bank** (randomized hand, direction, hold duration) — never freely generated by an LLM at runtime — so every challenge is guaranteed physically valid and unpredictable at the same time.

```python
import random

CHALLENGE_BANK = [
    {"instruction": "Touch your chin with your right palm and hold for 3 seconds, then say 'I authorize this payment'",
     "occlusion_type": "self_face_touch", "expected_occlusion_pct": 40, "motion": "static_hold"},
    {"instruction": "Turn your head completely to the left, hold 1s, then turn completely to the right",
     "occlusion_type": "extreme_profile", "expected_occlusion_pct": 30, "motion": "rapid_rotation"},
    {"instruction": "Pick up your phone and hold it over the left half of your face for 2 seconds",
     "occlusion_type": "foreground_object", "expected_occlusion_pct": 50, "motion": "object_hold"},
    {"instruction": "Wave your hand across your face left to right, then say 'security check complete'",
     "occlusion_type": "motion_sweep", "expected_occlusion_pct": 70, "motion": "high_velocity"},
]

def generate_occlusion_challenge():
    return random.choice(CHALLENGE_BANK)
```

## Part 2: The AI Analysis Layer

### Layer 1 — Semantic Verification (multimodal LLM)
Confirms *content*, never judges *authenticity*: did the user perform the action, count fingers, transcribe speech, confirm the occlusion actually happened.

```
You are a semantic verification system reviewing a video of a user performing a security challenge.
Challenge: "{instruction}"
1. Did the user perform the described physical action?
2. How many fingers did they hold up (if applicable)?
3. Transcribe exactly what they said.
4. Did the described occlusion actually occur?
Return JSON: {"action_performed": bool, "fingers_held": int|null, "spoken_text": str, "occlusion_confirmed": bool, "semantic_pass": bool}
```

### Layer 2 — Temporal Artifact Detection (custom CV pipeline)
Measures the specific degradation signature of real-time face-swap under occlusion/motion — the "Medusa Score":

| Metric | Measures | Initial threshold | Why it catches live swap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Landmark jitter | Frame-to-frame keypoint displacement | > 0.02 normalized units | Swap trackers are unstable, especially mid-occlusion |
| Edge flicker | Intensity variance at jaw/hairline boundary | > 15% pixel variance | Mask edges flicker when the tracked face is partly covered |
| Tracking loss | Consecutive frames with no face mesh detected | ≥ 3 frames | Real faces don't vanish; swap pipelines lose lock under heavy occlusion |
| Motion vector anomaly | Optical-flow discontinuity between frames | Variance > threshold | Real motion is smooth; swap pipelines insert synthetic frames that violate continuity |

Reference implementation: OpenCV + MediaPipe FaceMesh, Farneback optical flow, buildable in ~4 hours (see task list for the full class). Edge-flicker extraction should be replaced with a real jawline-boundary intensity-variance calculation before demo day — flag this as the first correctness item in QA, not as a limitation to disclose.

## Part 3: The Identity Graph — Tiered Network Analysis

**Hard links (deterministic, same-entity confidence):** phone (verified), email, PAN/Aadhaar, bank account number, card fingerprint.

**Soft links (behavioral, accumulate evidence):** device fingerprint (canvas/browser hash), IP address, failed-liveness events, geolocation proximity, near-duplicate email patterns.

### Tiered velocity windows

| Tier | Window | Catches | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — Bot-speed | Same device/IP + 3+ signals in 15 min | Scripted account creation, card testing | Immediate step-up challenge / hard block |
| 2 — Human-speed | Same device/IP + 2+ accounts in 24h | Manual mule account creation | Watchlist + increased friction |
| 3 — Long-game | Same fingerprint/IP across 3–7 days, different identities | Patient mule recruitment | Silent monitoring + risk-team notification |

Three tiers, not one magic window, because fraud operates at multiple speeds simultaneously — this is the key structural insight that differentiates Medusa from single-threshold velocity checks.

Reference implementation: SQLite + NetworkX for the demo (see task list). Design the schema so it can be lifted into Neo4j/TigerGraph for production scale without a rewrite — call this out in the pitch as a deliberate architectural choice, not a gap.

### Risk decision logic
```python
def assess_transaction(user_id, device_fingerprint, liveness_result, medusa_artifact_score):
    graph = MedusaIdentityGraph()
    if liveness_result == "FAILED":
        graph.add_edge(user_id, device_fingerprint, "soft", "failed_liveness", 1.0)
    tiers = graph.check_velocity_tiers(device_fingerprint)
    soft_score = graph.get_soft_link_score(device_fingerprint)
    total_risk = soft_score * 0.3 + medusa_artifact_score * 0.7
    if tiers["tier1_triggered"] or medusa_artifact_score > 5.0:
        return {"action": "BLOCK", "reason": "Bot-speed attack or high artifact score"}
    elif tiers["tier2_triggered"] or total_risk > 3.0:
        return {"action": "STEP_UP_CHALLENGE", "reason": "Suspicious pattern"}
    elif tiers["tier3_triggered"]:
        return {"action": "FLAG_FOR_REVIEW", "reason": "Long-term mule network pattern"}
    return {"action": "APPROVE", "reason": "Low risk"}
```

## Part 4: Demo Script

1. **Legitimate user (30s):** checkout → chin-touch challenge → Semantic 98%, Medusa Score 1.8, no graph triggers → **Approved.**
2. **Bot attack (45s):** 3 accounts, same device fingerprint, 10 minutes → Tier 1 fires, graph view shows a red cluster forming live → next transaction step-up/block.
3. **Live face-swap attack (45s):** pre-recorded face-swap tool attempts the occlusion challenge → Semantic 94% (driver performed it) but Medusa Score 7.2 (jitter + edge flicker + tracking loss during occlusion) → **Blocked**, routed to review.
4. **Slow mule network (30s):** 4 accounts over 4 days, same fingerprint, different emails → no Tier 1/2 trigger, Tier 3 fires → flagged, future friction increased.

## Part 5: Roadmap Beyond the Hackathon
Framed as deliberate staging, each with a concrete next milestone:
1. **Artifact thresholds:** ship hand-tuned thresholds for the demo; Week-2 milestone is a labeled dataset (real vs. live-swap video under occlusion) and a trained lightweight classifier replacing the hand-tuned scorer.
2. **Tracker robustness:** MediaPipe for the demo; evaluate a more lighting-robust keypoint model as a drop-in replacement once real-world traffic data exists.
3. **Graph scale:** NetworkX + SQLite for the demo; schema is Neo4j/TigerGraph-ready for production node counts.
4. **Automated action policy:** demo ships with BLOCK/STEP_UP/FLAG; production rollout pairs this with Razorpay's compliance/risk team to tune thresholds against real chargeback data.

## Part 7: The One-Sentence Pitch
"Medusa doesn't try to out-detect generative AI pixel-by-pixel — it forces attackers into physical maneuvers that break their face-swap pipelines, measures the exact degradation signature, and correlates it with a tiered identity graph that catches both bot-speed attacks and patient mule networks, all gated through Razorpay's payment flow."

## Why This Wins
1. **Grounded in published research** (GOTCHA, NYU) rather than an untested hypothesis — this is a credibility multiplier in Q&A.
2. **Precise, tiered velocity logic** instead of one arbitrary time window.
3. **Hard vs. soft link separation** — device fingerprints are treated as accumulating evidence, not instant proof, which is exactly how production fraud graphs behave.
4. **Fully buildable in a hackathon window** with standard, documented tools — no bespoke infra.
5. **A four-act demo** that shows every layer of the system catching a different, realistic attack pattern.
